## AI assistance and provider terms

OpenAI does not become PeopleBot's owner merely because OpenAI tools assisted development. Under OpenAI's [individual Terms of Use](https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/), as between the user and OpenAI and to the extent permitted by law, the user retains input rights and receives OpenAI's output rights, if any. Those terms also allow OpenAI to use content to provide, maintain, develop, improve, secure, and legally operate its services.

Personal-plan training controls affect applicable training use. OpenAI's [Codex data-controls documentation](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11369540-using-codex-with-your-chatgpt-plan) says Pro and Plus conversations may be used to improve models unless training is turned off. This repository neither states the account's current setting nor changes it. A PeopleBot GPL notice does not cancel separate provider agreements.

AI assistance does not itself settle copyrightability or third-party rights. The [U.S. Copyright Office's AI authorship summary](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html) explains that human-authored expression, creative arrangement, or modification can be protected, while prompts alone do not supply the required authorship. PeopleBot therefore preserves truthful provenance without certifying that every generated line is exclusively owned, copyrightable, or guaranteed noninfringing.

Repository instructions and checks help cooperating tools preserve notices. They cannot force an outside tool or person to comply and do not substitute for copyright law or license compliance.
